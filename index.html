The Least Cost Method (Matrix Minima)

  1. Balance the problem (add a dummy destination or source with zero costs if total supply ≠ total demand).
  2. Find the cell with the lowest transportation cost among the rows and columns still active.
  3. Allocate MIN(supply, demand) to that cell.
  4. Subtract the allocation from that row's supply and that column's demand.
  5. Cross out the row if its supply is 0, and the column if its demand is 0.
  6. Repeat until every supply and demand is satisfied.

Ties: when several cells share the lowest cost, the chosen tie-breaking rule is applied the same way every time. When one allocation exhausts both a row and a column, both are crossed out and the solution can end up degenerate.

The quantities you enter are the complete shipment plan. This tool calculates the row supplies, column demands, and total cost; it does not add or choose extra shipments.
Important: the Least Cost Method gives an initial basic feasible solution. It is usually better than the Northwest Corner Method, but it does not guarantee an optimal solution. Use the MODI (u-v) method or the stepping-stone method to test for optimality.
